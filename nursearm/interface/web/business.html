1. Problem

Medication delivery in care settings is repetitive, labour-intensive and easy to get wrong.

Facilities managing elderly or rehabilitating patients must repeatedly verify the right patient, the right dose and the right time. This consumes caregiver time, creates manual checking overhead and still leaves room for missed doses or poor adherence visibility.

2. Target Customer

MediPalm AI is designed for institutional buyers first.

Elderly care homes in Hong Kong

High medication frequency, repeated schedules and significant caregiver workload make this the strongest first segment.

Assisted living facilities

Residents need support but still benefit from a more independent and natural medication interaction.

Private hospitals

Controlled pilots can reduce low-complexity bedside medication handoff work while maintaining visibility.

Rehabilitation centers

Structured patient schedules make these environments suitable for guided dispensing workflows.

Home-care service providers

These operators need scalable medication adherence support across many patients and visits.

3. B2B Business Model

Hardware leasing plus software subscription creates predictable recurring revenue.

MediPalm AI is packaged as a care-ready robotics unit with software, support and adherence visibility. That is a better fit for healthcare procurement than selling a single capital-intensive robot with no service layer.

Facility Value

Reduce repetitive medication handoff workload

Staff spend less time on repeated low-complexity medication delivery and manual checking.

Patient Value

Safer and more natural interaction

Patients ask for medication conversationally and receive it only after open-palm confirmation.

Caregiver Value

Better visibility and accountability

Caregivers gain logs, alerts and a clearer view of whether medication was requested or dispensed.

5. Why Hong Kong

Hong Kong is a strong launch market because labour efficiency and clinical visibility matter at the same time.

The combination of dense healthcare systems, aging demographics, caregiver pressure and limited tolerance for opaque automation makes Hong Kong a strong environment for a visible, supervised robotics workflow.

6. Competitive Advantage

The product is differentiated by interaction design, not only by robotics hardware.

Natural palm gesture

A patient-ready handoff condition that is intuitive and visually verifiable.

Robotics + vision safety

Dispensing depends on real-world hand detection, not only a schedule timer.

Conversational medication support

The chatbot makes the workflow easier to understand for patients and staff.

Adherence logs

Medication events can be reviewed later for operational and accountability purposes.

Facility-friendly deployment

The business model fits facilities that already procure equipment, service and software together.

7. Go-To-Market

Start with elderly care homes and assisted living facilities.

This is the best first segment because these organisations manage many patients, repeated medication schedules and strong caregiver workload. The value proposition is operationally clear and easier to measure there than in a broad hospital rollout from day one.

Pilot

Deploy a small number of units in care homes and measure adherence workflow savings and staff acceptance.

Expand

Extend to assisted living and home-care partners once the workflow is validated.

Broaden

Use pilot data to approach hospitals and rehabilitation centres with a stronger procurement case.

8. 12-Month Roadmap

Prove the workflow first, then scale the platform.

Months 1–3

MVP completion: palm detection, robotic dispensing, chatbot and medication logging.

Months 4–6

Pilot with care homes and assisted living partners in Hong Kong.

Months 7–9

Operational and regulatory validation, plus caregiver dashboard refinement.

Months 10–12

Hospital integration discussions, broader deployment model and enterprise reporting.

9. Impact Metrics

Judge the system by workflow outcomes, not only by technical novelty.

Caregiver time saved

Minutes of repeated medication handoff work reduced per shift.

Missed-dose reduction

Improvement in adherence completion and visibility of pending doses.

Auditability

Percentage of medication events with clear logs and timestamped handoff trace.

Patient independence

Increase in successful guided medication interactions without direct caregiver intervention.
